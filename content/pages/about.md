## What FounderCalc is

FounderCalc is a free collection of business calculators for indie founders,
freelancers and early-stage SaaS builders — the questions that come up
constantly when you run a small digital business and need an accurate answer
in ten seconds rather than a spreadsheet in twenty minutes.

Every calculator covers one specific question: what Stripe actually pays out
on a $2,400 invoice, how many months of runway a given burn rate buys, what
hourly rate a $95,000 income target implies once non-billable time and
self-employment tax are accounted for. Each tool sits alongside a written
explanation of the formula it uses, the assumptions behind it, and the
benchmarks that make the result meaningful.

## How the numbers are produced

**Formulas.** Every calculator implements a standard, publicly documented
formula — the same arithmetic used in accounting texts, investor updates and
SaaS operating handbooks. Where a metric has more than one accepted
definition (LTV computed on revenue versus gross profit, churn measured by
logo versus revenue), the page states which one the tool uses and why the
distinction matters.

**Rates and fees.** Processor fees, tax rates and similar third-party figures
are taken from each provider's own published pricing page, and the page links
back to that source so you can confirm the current figure yourself. Worked
examples are computed at build time from the same rate constants the
calculator uses, so an example can never drift out of sync with the rate
quoted next to it.

**Benchmarks.** Figures described as typical or healthy — a 3:1 LTV:CAC ratio,
25–40% employer on-costs, 50–70% freelance utilisation — are widely used
industry rules of thumb, presented as ranges rather than targets. They are
context for reading your own number, not standards your business is failing
to meet.

**Corrections.** Third-party pricing changes, and mistakes happen. If a figure
on this site is wrong or out of date, [tell me](/contact/) and it gets fixed;
corrections are treated as a priority over new tools.

## What the results are not

Calculator output on this site is **information, not advice**. Nothing here is
financial, tax, accounting, investment or legal advice, and none of it accounts
for the specifics of your jurisdiction, your entity type or your circumstances.

Tax and payroll tools in particular are planning estimates: rates, thresholds
and allowances differ by country and change between tax years. Use them to
decide how much to set aside, then confirm the actual liability with your
accountant or tax authority before you file anything. Valuation and investment
models are only as reliable as the forecasts you feed them — treat their output
as one input to a decision, not the decision. The full position is in the
[Terms of Use](/terms/).

## Your data stays in your browser

Every calculator runs entirely as client-side JavaScript. The numbers you type
are processed in your browser and are never transmitted to this site or
anywhere else — there is no account, no login, and no analytics script
recording what you enter. The only request your browser makes is the ordinary
one for the page itself.

That is a structural property of how the site is built rather than a promise
about how data is handled after collection: there is no server-side
calculation, so there is nothing to collect. What little data does get
processed — server logs, and the advertising described in the
[privacy policy](/privacy/) — is set out there in full.

## Why it exists

These calculations are not difficult. Doing them from scratch every time is
friction, and friction is why founders guess at numbers they could know. A
single-purpose page that opens instantly, works without an account, explains
its own formula and remembers nothing about you removes that friction — and
costs nothing to keep running, which is why it can stay free.

The site is funded by advertising. It has no paid tier, sells no data, and
gates no calculator behind an email address.
